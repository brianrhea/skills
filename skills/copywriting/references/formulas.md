# Copywriting formulas

A formula is scaffolding. It tells you what order to say things in. It does not tell you what to say; that comes from the research. Fill every slot with something a customer said or did. If a slot has nothing behind it, write "NEEDS ANSWER" and leave it for the user.

## Which one to use

| Formula | Best for | Reader's stage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BAB | Short pieces: ads, cold emails, onboarding emails | Not looking yet, or just starting |
| SLAY | LinkedIn posts and other story-led posts | Not looking yet |
| The 4 Ps | Landing pages, sales emails, longer ads | Comparing options, or close to deciding |
| QUEST | Landing pages and sales emails for a specific kind of buyer | Comparing options |
| VFCA | LinkedIn posts and opinion pieces | Not looking yet |
| PASTOR | Long sales pages and sales emails with an offer | Close to deciding |

When the piece matters, draft the lead section two ways and let the user choose.

## BAB: Before, After, Bridge

| Slot | What goes in it | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before | The reader's situation today, in their words | The strongest push |
| After | What life looks like once it is fixed | The strongest pull |
| Bridge | How they get from one to the other | The product, and the first step of the job |

Keep "Before" to something they would say out loud. The Bridge is one idea, with no feature list.

## SLAY: Story, Lesson, Advice, You

| Slot | What goes in it | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story | Something that happened | The user's own experience, or a customer's switch story from an interview, with the customer's permission and no identifying detail |
| Lesson | What it showed | The finding the story illustrates |
| Advice | What to do about it | One instruction the reader can act on today |
| You | Turn to the reader | A question about their own situation |

The story has to be true. Ask the user for one if the research has none. Never write a made-up anecdote, and never present a customer's story as the user's own.

## The 4 Ps: Picture, Promise, Proof, Push

| Slot | What goes in it | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picture | A scene the reader recognises | A struggling moment from an interview, or the "after" customers described |
| Promise | What the product will do for them | The core job, and the top pull |
| Proof | Why they should believe it | Verified quotes, counts from the research, numbers the user confirms |
| Push | The ask | The first step of the job, with the top anxiety answered beside it |

## QUEST: Qualify, Understand, Educate, Stimulate, Transition

Usually credited to Michel Fortin.

| Slot | What goes in it | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualify | Who this is for, so the wrong reader leaves | The situation customers were in when they started looking, not a job title |
| Understand | Show you know the problem | Pushes, quoted |
| Educate | How this kind of problem gets solved | The job map: the steps, in order |
| Stimulate | Why it is worth doing | Pulls, and what customers said changed |
| Transition | From reader to customer | A small next step |

## VFCA: Visceral opener, Fresh perspective, Challenge, Anaphora

| Slot | What goes in it | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visceral opener | A first line the reader feels | The most vivid thing a customer said, word for word |
| Fresh perspective | A way of seeing it they have not considered | The reframing the research supports: the job behind the request, or what people really use instead |
| Challenge | Something to question or do | A habit the research shows is holding people back |
| Anaphora | A close built on repetition | Three or four short lines that open with the same words |

Anaphora is a rhythm, such as "You don't need more features. You don't need a bigger team. You don't need another dashboard." Use it once, at the end.

## PASTOR: Problem, Amplify, Solution, Testimony, Offer, Response

Usually credited to Ray Edwards.

| Slot | What goes in it | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem | The problem in the reader's words | Pushes |
| Amplify | What it costs to leave it alone | What customers said it cost them: the workaround, the hours, the embarrassing moment |
| Solution | How it gets solved | The product, following the job map |
| Testimony | Someone like them saying it worked | Customer quotes the user has permission to publish |
| Offer | Exactly what they get | Supplied by the user |
| Response | What to do now | One clear action |

Amplify with what customers reported. Do not inflate it. If the research has no publishable testimony, mark the slot "NEEDS ANSWER"; research quotes are not testimonials.
