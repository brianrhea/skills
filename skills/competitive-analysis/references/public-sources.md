# Public sources

What each source tells you, and how to treat it. Rules on automated access change, and this is not legal advice. When in doubt, read less and ask the user for an export.

## Read freely: what the company publishes

| Page | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Home page | Who it is for and the main promise, in the words they chose |
| Pricing | What they charge for, how tiers are divided, what they treat as premium |
| Features or product pages | What they think matters most, by what comes first |
| Changelog or release notes | What they are investing in, and how fast |
| Documentation | What the product really does, and its limits |
| Customer stories | Which customers they want more of |
| Careers | Where they are hiring, which hints at where they are heading |
| Status page | How reliable they have been |
| Public code repositories | For open products: activity, and what users ask for in issues |
| Company filings | For public companies: revenue, customers and risks in their own words |
| Archived copies of any of these | What the page said on an earlier date |

## Read lightly: a page or two, never in bulk

Review sites, app store listings and public question-and-answer sites. Their terms forbid automated collection. Reading a single public page the user points you to is different from gathering hundreds. Prefer the user's own export or pasted text.

Treat ratings and review counts with caution. Many reviews are written because a vendor asked or offered a reward.

## Do not use unless the user supplies it

Discussion forums and social networks that require an account or approval for automated access, private communities, and paid data services. If the user has an export, a licence or approved access, work from what they give you.

## When a page will not load

Record the address, the date and what happened under "Not reachable". That is a finding. Do not retry through another address, an archive of a blocked page, or a different way of fetching it.

## For every claim

Record the address, the date you read it, the date on the page if it has one, and whose voice it is: the company's, a customer's, or your inference.
