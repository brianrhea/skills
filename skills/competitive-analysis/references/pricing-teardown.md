# Reading a pricing page

Work through these and record each answer with the page address and date. Write "not stated publicly" where the page is silent.

1. **What is being charged for.** Per person, per unit of use, per feature, a flat fee, or a mix. This is the most revealing line on the page, because it shows what the company believes customers value.
2. **The tiers.** Names, prices, billing period, and the annual discount if shown.
3. **What separates the tiers.** Which limits and features push a customer up a level. The item that forces the first upgrade tells you what they think a growing customer cannot live without.
4. **What needs a sales call.** Anything behind "contact us", and who that implies they want to talk to.
5. **Free plan or trial.** What it includes, how long it lasts, and whether a card is needed.
6. **Extra charges.** Setup, add-ons, overage, support, and anything in the small print.
7. **Who the page is written for.** The words in the tier names and descriptions: "teams", "startups", "enterprise".
8. **What has changed.** If an archived copy exists, compare it and give both dates.

## What to make of it

- A charging unit that works against how customers get value is an opening. Per-person pricing for a tool whose value is sharing is the classic example.
- Features that sit in the top tier and that your research says customers barely mention are signs of over-serving.
- Prices vary by country and by who is looking. Say where you read from if the page shows a currency or region.
