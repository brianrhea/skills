# Reviews of the user's own product

**Good for:** the language customers use, what they compared the product with, and what they tell other buyers. Useful raw material for copywriting.

**Distorts:** reviews cluster at the extremes, and many are written because the vendor asked or offered a reward. Star averages and review counts say little.

**How to read them**
- Work from the user's own export or pasted text. Do not collect reviews in bulk from public pages.
- Middling reviews, two to four stars, carry the most detail about what works and what does not.
- Capture exact phrases first and sort them afterwards into the problem before, what made it worse, the fix, and what changed. That order follows the review-mining method Joanna Wiebe teaches for copy.
- Phrases that many reviewers repeat are candidates for headlines. Record them with `"kind": "pull"` or `"push"`.
- "I switched from" stories are the most useful reviews. Record what they left and why.
- Keep the rating and date with each record. Quote a sentence or two at most, and use the reviewer's role, never their name.
